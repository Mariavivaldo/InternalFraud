    title: Fed fines former First Security Bancorp insider Rush F. Harding III $100,000 over marked-up bond sales to the bank
    summary: The November 19 consent order says he routed municipal bonds from affiliate Crews & Associates to First Security Bank at marked-up prices from 2017 to 2021.
    detail: >-
      The Federal Reserve Board announced on November 19, 2026 a consent cease-and-desist order and
      a $100,000 civil money penalty against Rush F. Harding III, a former institution-affiliated
      party of First Security Bancorp, the Searcy, Arkansas bank holding company that owns First
      Security Bank and the Little Rock broker-dealer Crews & Associates, which Harding formerly
      led as chief executive. According to the order, from 2017 to 2021 Harding used other
      broker-dealers as intermediaries to sell municipal securities from Crews to First Security
      Bank at marked-up prices, and he earned commissions through third parties. This went against
      a 2015 agreement between the bank and the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis that set
      conditions for such affiliate trades under Sections 23A and 23B of the Federal Reserve Act and
      Regulation W, including that Crews buy and resell a security to the bank on the same day and
      at the same price. The Board cites breach of fiduciary duty and violation of Regulation W,
      and the order says the conduct caused the bank a financial loss; the amount is not given in
      the reports seen. The penalty is payable within 60 days. Harding consented without admitting
      or denying the allegations. The Fed corrected the release on the same day to describe the
      action as a consent cease-and-desist order rather than a prohibition, so it does not bar him
      from banking. This is separate from a 2021 SEC settlement in which Harding paid a $100,000
      penalty over a 2015 municipal bond tender. The Fed pages could not be opened from this
      session, so these details come from them as shown in search results.

    summary: Police say the 33-year-old planner coached clients to pass long-term care assessments using walkers, wheelchairs and diapers; 16 people were sent to prosecutors.
    detail: >-
      Busan Nambu Police Station announced on November 20, 2026 that it has arrested an insurance
      planner identified as A, 33, and referred him and 15 accomplices, some of them related to
      one another, to prosecutors on charges under the Insurance Fraud Prevention Act; the others
      were referred without detention. Police say that between June 2025 and September 2026 the
      clients took out policies, paid premiums only through the 90-day waiting period, and then
      deceived doctors and National Health Insurance Service assessors into granting them
      long-term care grades by pretending to have cognitive impairment, using walkers, wheelchairs
      and diapers and rehearsing for home visits. The planner allegedly told them in detail how to
      obtain the grades. The group is accused of collecting about ₩1.5 billion in insurance
      payouts. The investigation began with a complaint from an insurer; police checked how the
      suspects actually lived and analysed about 190,000 messages between the planner and the
      policyholders to show the scheme was planned in advance. The reports seen do not name the
      planner's agency or the insurers. These are allegations. The articles could not be opened
      from this session, so these details come from them as shown in search results.
