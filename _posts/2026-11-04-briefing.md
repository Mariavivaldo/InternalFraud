    title: All 47 jeonse loans handled by one Seoul bank employee ended in guarantee defaults, lawmaker says, and calls for an FSS investigation

    detail: >-
      At a press conference at the National Assembly on November 4, Han Chang-min, a lawmaker
      from the Social Democratic Party who sits on the National Policy Committee, and the civic
      group People's Solidarity for Participatory Democracy (PSPD) presented internal data first
      raised during this autumn's parliamentary audit. The data show that from May to December
      2021, one employee at a commercial bank branch in Gangbuk-gu, Seoul, handled 47 jeonse (lump-sum
      lease deposit) loans totalling ₩10.65 billion, and that every one of them ended in a guarantee
      default. Twenty of the loans, worth ₩4.81 billion, were for homes owned by the same landlord.
      Six files had no appraisal report, seven borrowers had no income, and in 38 cases both the
      borrower's address and the home were outside Gangbuk-gu, where the branch is. The group says
      the bank found signs of possible jeonse fraud and missing documents, but its internal audit
      rated its internal controls and the employee's work "average" and ordered only training.
      Park Hyun-geun, a lawyer on PSPD's executive committee, said the case points to structural
      problems in loan screening and internal controls, beyond one employee's misconduct. Han said
      the Financial Supervisory Service is carrying out a routine inspection of the bank and urged
      it to check how the loans came to be concentrated in one branch and one employee. The group
      also asked the Financial Services Commission and the FSS to review problem jeonse loans across
      the sector, establish the banks' responsibility and help victims. The bank and the employee
      are not named, and no wrongdoing by the employee has been established. These are a
      lawmaker's claims based on internal data, and the reports carry no response from the FSS or the
      bank. The
