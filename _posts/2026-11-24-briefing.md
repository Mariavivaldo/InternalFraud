      which a judge earlier froze their assets. The credit union was placed into conservatorship

    category: Insider & employee fraud
    title: Florida mortgage loan officer pleads guilty to a bank fraud conspiracy against the bank he worked for
    summary: Richard Exposito, 64, admits submitting loan applications backed by falsified pay stubs from 2016 to 2019 and agrees to forfeit $18,648.37 in commissions.
    detail: >-
      The U.S. Attorney's Office for the Middle District of Florida said Richard Exposito, 64, of
      North Bay Village, Florida, a licensed mortgage loan officer, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to
      commit bank fraud. From December 2016 to July 2019 he worked with a document preparer,
      Kiomy Torres, on a mortgage fraud scheme aimed at the bank where he worked. He took client
      referrals from Torres, told her which clients lacked the income or assets to qualify and what
      figures they needed, and then submitted applications backed by falsified pay stubs and other
      documents she supplied, collecting commissions on the loans that were approved. He faces up
      to 30 years in federal prison and agreed to forfeit $18,648.37 in proceeds; no sentencing
      date has been set. Torres pleaded guilty to the same charge on May 5, 2026 and is due to be
      sentenced on December 16. The bank was not named. The FHFA Office of Inspector General, HUD
      OIG and the FBI investigated the case. The release could not be opened from this session, so
      these details come from it as shown in search results.
