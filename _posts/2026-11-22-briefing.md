    title: Maryland insurance agent sentenced over 21 fake life insurance applications that earned him commissions
    summary: Corrie Dewayne Alston of Bowie gets 30 days of home detention and five years' probation; he took $5,122.87 in commissions from Senior Life Insurance Company.
    detail: >-
      Corrie Dewayne Alston, 52, of Bowie, Maryland, was sentenced on November 18, 2026 in Prince
      George's County Circuit Court by Judge Nicholas E. Rattal to 30 days of home detention
      followed by five years of probation, the office of Maryland Attorney General Anthony G. Brown
      announced. He had pleaded guilty to felony theft scheme and felony insurance fraud. While
      working as an IT specialist for the federal government, Alston also worked as a licensed
      Maryland insurance agent selling policies for Senior Life Insurance Company through the Don
      Pope Insurance Agency. Investigators found that he submitted 21 life insurance applications
      for people who did not exist and collected $5,122.87 in commissions on those policies.
      According to the Attorney General's office, he told investigators that he chose Hispanic
      surnames because he believed the insurer would assume the applicants were undocumented and
      would not check their Social Security numbers. He has paid full restitution to Senior Life,
      and he could face five more years of incarceration if he violates probation. Reports differ
      slightly on his age (51 or 52) and on the date of his guilty plea. The Attorney General's
      release and the articles could not be opened from this session, so these details come from
      them as shown in search results.
