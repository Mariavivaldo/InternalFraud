    title: Florida insurance agency's agent-in-charge arrested for letting a barred agent sell fake coverage
    summary: Alberto Villaruel allegedly knew Yaudat Antonio Ohan was unlicensed and let him collect premiums for policies that were never active.
    detail: >-
      Florida Chief Financial Officer Blaise Ingoglia announced on October 27 the arrests of
      Yaudat Antonio Ohan and Alberto Villaruel by the Department of Financial Services' criminal
      investigators. The case began when a construction company submitted two certificates of
      insurance to a department compliance officer. The insurer said the policies had never been
      active, although a $10,500 premium had been paid to Ohan. Ohan has been permanently barred
      from holding an insurance agent license since 2002, but he worked as an agent at
      Villaruel's agency. Investigators say Villaruel, the agency's licensed agent-in-charge, knew
      Ohan was unlicensed and let him collect premiums without providing valid coverage. Ohan was
      arrested on September 28 and is charged with racketeering conspiracy, organized scheme to
      defraud, grand theft, uttering a forged instrument, unlawful use of a telecommunications
      device, transacting insurance without a license and insurance funds diversion. Villaruel was
      arrested on October 20 and is charged with grand theft, organized scheme to defraud and
      racketeering conspiracy. The Miami-Dade State Attorney's Office is prosecuting.

    title: Financial Services Commission sanctions 29 insurance workers over insurance fraud, cancelling 19 registrations
    summary: 28 planners and a claims adjuster were sanctioned for staged accidents, false hospital stays and falsified records; one planner took ₩489m from eight insurers.
    detail: >-
      The Financial Services Commission made public on October 29 sanctions against 29 people in
      the insurance industry, 28 insurance planners and one claims adjuster, over their
      involvement in insurance fraud found in Financial Supervisory Service inspections. After a
      hearing on August 26, the sanctions were decided on October 16. 19 registrations were
      cancelled, 7 people were suspended for 180 days and 3 for 90 days. Staged traffic accidents
      were the most common type (16 people), followed by false hospitalization, falsified medical
      records, false or excessive diagnoses and breaches of the duty of disclosure. In the largest
      case, a planner conspired with several people to stage accidents and collected ₩489.48
      million from eight non-life insurers. The power to sanction insurance workers for fraud
      dates from a 2014 amendment to the Insurance Business Act. The commission's own release
      could not be checked, so this item relies on press reports.

    title: Suji Shinhyup chairman sent to prosecutors over ₩410m retirement payout to an executive accused of harassment
    summary: Police say chairman Lee Gi-chan caused the credit union a loss by paying an honorary-retirement bonus its rules did not allow.
    detail: >-
      Yongin Seobu Police Station sent Lee Gi-chan, chairman of Suji Shinhyup, a credit union in
      Yongin, to the Suwon District Prosecution Office on a charge of occupational breach of
      trust, CBS NoCut News reported on October 28. Police allege that Lee knew in 2019 that an
      executive had sexually harassed an employee, after about 30 staff demanded the executive's
      dismissal. An extraordinary board meeting in January 2020 instead approved the executive's
      honorary retirement, and about ₩410 million in honorary-retirement pay and other payments
      were made that internal rules did not allow, causing the credit union a loss. Police found
      the breach-of-trust charge made out. The Shinhyup federation audited the matter but
      imposed no discipline.
