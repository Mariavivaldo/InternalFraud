    detail: >-
      Richard Marion Blosser, 63, of Missoula, Montana, the owner and chief compliance officer of
      Pathfinder Wealth Advisors, a registered investment adviser based in Los Angeles, was indicted
      by a federal grand jury in Montana on November 18, 2026 on 13 counts of wire fraud, six counts
      of money laundering and six counts of concealing or transferring assets in connection with his
      personal bankruptcy. He has pleaded not guilty. Prosecutors allege he raised money from
      clients, friends and relatives through Pathfinder and a second entity, Sanctuary Partners LLC,
      promising to invest it, diverted it to personal expenses, forged documents showing the money
      was invested and earning returns, took money from a deceased client's trusts and obtained
      loans with fraudulent documents. ThinkAdvisor and Financial Advisor magazine put the alleged
      losses at $25 million; the Bozeman Daily Chronicle reports victims' losses of more than $20
      million. Blosser was formerly a Wells Fargo managing director, was also registered through UBS
      and others, and accepted a FINRA bar in 2022, but kept running his advisory firm afterward. The
      IRS Criminal Investigation and the FBI investigated. No trial date was found. The release and
      articles could not be opened from this session, so these details come from them as shown in

    summary: Wong (黃錦輝) helped clients claim they had left Hong Kong for good to withdraw MPF savings early; three clients received suspended sentences.
    detail: >-
      Deputy District Judge 溫紹明 on November 26, 2026 jailed Wong (黃錦輝), a former
      insurance agent and Mandatory Provident Fund intermediary, for 40 months after he was convicted
      at trial of four counts of conspiracy to defraud and two counts of using a false instrument in
      a case brought by the ICAC. Between July 2021 and November 2022 he helped clients open or
      transfer MPF accounts with Sun Life Trustee and then apply for early withdrawal on the false
      ground that they had permanently left Hong Kong, including for the Mainland, using false
      statutory declarations and supporting documents such as leases or employment contracts. About
      HK$4.6 million was withdrawn, and reports say he charged the clients fees for his help. The judge called him the
      instigator, said he acted for money and that the scheme damaged the MPF system and the public
      interest. Three clients who had pleaded guilty were sentenced to 12 to 16 months in jail,
      suspended for two years; a fourth was acquitted. The ICAC first charged the case in 2024. The
      articles could not be opened from this session, so these details come from them as shown in
      search results.
