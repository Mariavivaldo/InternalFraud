    detail: >-
      The National Credit Union Administration's October 2026 enforcement release, dated October
      30, announces two conviction-based prohibition orders. Ian Edward Moore, a former employee
      of Heartland Credit Union in Springfield, Illinois, was convicted in the US District Court
      for the Central District of Illinois of credit union theft or embezzlement and bank fraud.
      According to the order, which is dated October 19, 2026, he was convicted and sentenced on or
      about February 16, 2026. Haley M. Snodgrass, a former employee of Doches Credit Union in
      Nacogdoches, Texas, was convicted in the Eastern District of Texas of credit union theft,
      embezzlement or misapplication. Her order is dated August 12, 2026. Trade press reports that
      she joined the credit union as a teller in 2016 and later became a branch manager. She
      admitted taking about $281,098, and in July 2025 she was sentenced to 33 months in prison
      and ordered to pay $330,351 in restitution. Both orders permanently bar them from working
      for any federally insured depository institution. The NCUA pages could not be opened from
      this session, so these details come from search results for the official pages and from
      trade-press coverage.

      Amanda Larue Chandler, 39, of Houtzdale, the Mid Penn Bank teller charged by Pennsylvania
      State Police with taking about $32,300 from nine customers, waived her right to a preliminary
      hearing at Centralized Court before Magisterial District Judge J. Michael Morris in
      Kylertown, The Progress reported. The waiver sends the case to the Clearfield County Court of
      Common Pleas. Bail was set the same day at $100,000 unsecured, and her formal arraignment is
      scheduled for November 18. The Progress lists the charges as 22 counts of theft by unlawful
      taking, graded as third-degree felonies, and 22 counts of forgery, graded as first-degree
      misdemeanors. Police say she used customers' account details at the drive-thru to make out
      withdrawal slips in their names between December 2025 and August 2026. The bank has
      reimbursed the customers. No court or police release was found, so this item relies on local
      press.

      Follow-up reports on the KB Securities case first reported on October 30 add new details.
      ESG Economy reports that the firm's internal audit is under way and that the company says it
      has reported the matter to the Financial Supervisory Service. SmartBiz, citing a company
      official, reports that the employee is believed to have used a personal friendship with the
      client to get delegated access to the client's trading channels. The trades were
      infrequent and used accounts in family members' names and personal contact channels, which
      the firm says made it hard for monitoring to flag them in advance. The employee has been
      suspended from duty and barred from the premises, and a complaint has been filed with the
      police. The amount involved, how long the trading went on, and whether police have opened a
      formal investigation are not yet confirmed. Both outlets note that this is not the firm's
      first case: in 2018 an employee withdrew about ₩360 million from some 25 dormant client
      accounts. The employee received a sanction equivalent to dismissal, and KB Securities
      received an institutional caution. No official release on the new case was found. These
      details come from press reports, and their publication dates could not be confirmed.
