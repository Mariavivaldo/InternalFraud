      prejudgment interest of $101,252 and a civil penalty of $236,451, and barring him from the
      securities industry. Trade press, which reported the case on November 23 and 24, says
      Raymond James Financial Services fired him in June 2025 and that he also worked for a credit
      union whose members were offered investment services through Raymond James; FINRA barred him
      in October 2025 after he refused to cooperate with its inquiry, and Raymond James paid
      $413,000 to settle a customer claim. The complaint does not name Raymond James. The filings
      and articles could not be opened from this session, so these details come from them as shown

  - id: kr-fsc-planner-disqualification-rules

    title: FSC proposes barring people with insurance-fraud convictions from registering as insurance planners
    summary: Draft decree amendments widen the laws that disqualify planners and let agencies pay fines instead of business suspensions; comments run to January 4.
    detail: >-
      The Financial Services Commission announced on November 24, 2026 draft amendments to the
      Insurance Business Act enforcement decree and the rules on inspection and sanctions of
      financial institutions, following the amended Insurance Business Act promulgated in September.
      The drafts widen the "financial laws" used to decide whether someone is disqualified from
      working as an insurance planner or other sales agent: in addition to the Insurance Business
      Act and the Financial Consumer Protection Act, convictions under the Special Act on the
      Prevention of Insurance Fraud, Article 347 of the Criminal Act (fraud using insurance
      contracts only) and the Act on the Regulation of Conducting Fundraising Business without
      Permission would also count. Insurance agencies and brokers could pay a fine instead of a
      business suspension when 150 or fewer of their planners, or no more than 15 percent of them,
      took part in a violation, or when planners only assisted on the agency's instructions; the
      fine is capped at the equivalent of six months' suspension. The proposals are open for
      comment from November 25 to January 4, 2027 and are due to take effect on March 9, 2027. The
      commission's own release could not be opened from this session, so this item relies on press
      reports as shown in search results.
